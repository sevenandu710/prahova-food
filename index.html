A short introduction to Romanian food

Romanian cooking is hearty, generous and built for sharing. It grew from the Carpathian mountains, the Danube plains and centuries of crossing influences, so you will find hints of Balkan, Hungarian, Turkish and Austrian kitchens on the same table.

Meals are social. Families gather on Sundays, friends grill outdoors on warm days, and holidays like Easter and Christmas centre on slow-cooked dishes and sweet bread. Our home is named after the Prahova Valley, a mountain region known for its hospitality.

The most famous street food is mici (said "meech"): garlicky grilled minced-meat rolls, served hot with mustard and fresh bread. We make ours the traditional way and grill them to order.
